# Beehive deck family design

Date: 2026-08-11  
Status: draft for review  
Owner: Kenneth / Beehive Strategy  
Scope: PowerPoint deliverables only. Website work is explicitly out of scope for this phase because it will be handled separately in TRAE Work CN.

## Objective

Create a modular PowerPoint sales system for Beehive Strategy that:

1. preserves one consistent Beehive narrative across all offers
2. supports focused selling for different buyer groups
3. uses the Obsidian vault as the source of truth for messaging, positioning, and offer logic
4. allows a second wave of more independent offer decks after the shared system is stable

## Locked decisions

These decisions are treated as approved assumptions for this spec so execution can move forward without further clarification:

- Build `Option A` first: modular shared deck family.
- Build `Option B` second: more independent buyer-specific decks derived from the modular family.
- Use the existing Obsidian vault under `0 - New Beehive/obsidian/Beehive Strategy` as the narrative source of truth.
- Focus only on PowerPoint deliverables in this track.
- TokenHub must serve both technical buyers and commercial buyers, including digital marketing agencies and CMOs.
- Buyer targeting is mixed, not identical across all offers.

## Strategic frame

Beehive should not present itself as four unrelated businesses. The deck family should position Beehive as one company with one delivery philosophy and four commercial entry points.

The umbrella narrative is:

`Beehive embeds practical AI into real business workflows and delivers outcomes, not just tools.`

The four offer pillars underneath that umbrella are:

1. `Company Brain / Conversational BI`
2. `FDE`
3. `TokenHub`
4. `Training`

## Phase structure

## Phase 1

Build the modular shared deck family first.

Outputs:

1. `Deck A1` — Company Brain / Conversational BI
2. `Deck A2` — FDE
3. `Deck A3` — TokenHub
4. `Deck A4` — Training
5. `Deck A5` — Master corporate deck covering all four offers

Purpose:

- establish one shared Beehive story
- align tone, claims, proof logic, and visual identity
- create a reusable slide system for later variants

## Phase 2

Create more independent buyer-specific variants after Phase 1 is complete.

Illustrative outputs:

- FDE enterprise leadership version
- FDE transformation sponsor version
- TokenHub CTO / AI lead version
- TokenHub CMO / digital marketing agency version
- Training HR / L&D version

Purpose:

- sharpen fit for distinct conversations
- adapt emphasis without breaking the core Beehive identity

## Offer architecture

## 1. Company Brain / Conversational BI

Role in portfolio:

- flagship business-outcome offer
- primary proof of Beehive’s “answers, not work” philosophy
- strongest bridge into managed service revenue

Primary buyers:

- CEO
- COO
- business heads
- department leaders

Core promise:

`Your company brain — online 24/7, ask in your chat, get answers in 2 seconds.`

Deck emphasis:

- business outcomes
- adoption advantage of IM-native delivery
- managed service model
- 15 years of practitioner credibility

## 2. FDE

Role in portfolio:

- premium embedded-delivery narrative
- reframes Beehive as a practical, lightweight, mid-market alternative to large-vendor AI deployment models

Primary buyers:

- enterprise leadership
- transformation sponsors
- IT and business joint owners

Core promise:

`FDE-grade delivery without enterprise-vendor overhead.`

Deck emphasis:

- why embedded AI delivery matters now
- why deployment, not just model quality, is the bottleneck
- how Beehive delivers “embedded AI in workflow” through IM-native architecture and managed service
- how Beehive sits on the exponential reuse curve

## 3. TokenHub

Role in portfolio:

- AI operating layer and commercial enabler
- connects infrastructure, model routing, creative production, and campaign execution

Primary buyers:

- CTO
- AI lead
- product / engineering
- CMO
- digital marketing agency owner
- performance marketing lead

Core promise:

`One gateway for multiple Chinese frontier models, packaged as a working production layer for AI execution.`

Deck emphasis:

- unified model access and cost control
- multi-model routing and vendor flexibility
- creative production for image and video workflows
- agency and campaign use cases
- Beehive as the service layer on top of the gateway

Important narrative constraint:

TokenHub must not be framed as raw token resale alone. It should be framed as:

- AI model gateway
- production backbone
- creative ops engine
- managed service opportunity

## 4. Training

Role in portfolio:

- capability-building offer
- lead-generation entry point
- lower-friction commercial entry for teams not yet ready for platform or FDE deployment

Primary buyers:

- HR
- L&D
- department heads
- business owners

Core promise:

`Practical AI transformation training that produces real outputs, not passive slide consumption.`

Deck emphasis:

- hands-on methodology
- clear tier ladder
- output-driven workshops
- corporate implementation path back into core Beehive services

## 5. Master deck

Role in portfolio:

- umbrella company deck
- investor / partner / corporate overview
- cross-offer conversation deck

Primary use cases:

- first meeting where the buyer does not yet know which offer fits
- partnership conversations
- strategic introductions
- senior executive overview meetings

Core promise:

`Beehive is a practical AI outcomes company with four connected ways to help clients deploy, operate, and scale AI.`

## Shared narrative spine

Every Phase 1 deck should share the same core structure, even if slide counts differ.

Required common sections:

1. Why now
2. Why this problem matters
3. Why current alternatives fall short
4. Why Beehive
5. Offer-specific solution
6. Delivery model
7. Proof / ROI / commercial logic
8. Next step / CTA

Shared company truths that should appear across the family:

- Beehive sells outcomes, not commoditized effort
- Beehive combines practitioner experience with AI delivery
- Beehive is managed service oriented, not just software oriented
- Beehive focuses on practical deployment, not abstract AI theory
- Beehive is strong in Hong Kong, cross-border China, and multilingual business contexts

## Visual system

The deck family should visually feel like one system, not five unrelated files.

Visual rules:

- reuse the current Beehive deck aesthetic as the primary reference
- preserve a premium, executive, dark-theme presentation style unless an existing approved deck clearly establishes a stronger house style
- use one shared title system, colour system, section divider style, and data-card style
- allow light offer-specific accents, but not separate visual identities
- keep layouts editable and presentation-friendly, not overdesigned

Offer-specific visual cues:

- Company Brain: core brand treatment
- FDE: slightly more enterprise / strategic tone
- TokenHub: slightly more technical / ecosystem / production tone
- Training: slightly more educational / ladder / pathway tone

## Editorial rules

- no jargon-first framing
- business value before technical explanation
- every deck must explain the buyer problem in plain language within the first 3-4 slides
- avoid unsupported numeric claims unless they already exist in trusted internal source notes
- do not let TokenHub become a commodity pricing deck
- do not let Training overshadow the core commercial offers
- do not let FDE sound like a big-consulting copycat narrative

## Reuse model

The family should use reusable slide blocks.

Shared reusable blocks:

- Beehive intro
- why now / market shift
- why Beehive
- delivery model
- commercial ladder
- CTA

Offer-specific reusable blocks:

- Company Brain proof and IM-native slides
- FDE market and deployment slides
- TokenHub model gateway and creative production slides
- Training curriculum and workshop slides

This should make later Phase 2 buyer variants cheaper and faster to produce.

## Risks

1. `Offer overlap risk`  
   Company Brain, FDE, and TokenHub can blur together if each deck is not sharply framed.

2. `TokenHub commoditization risk`  
   If the deck leans too far into pricing and models, it will feel like low-margin token resale.

3. `Training distraction risk`  
   Training can consume too much attention relative to core business if not framed correctly.

4. `Deck sprawl risk`  
   Too many slide variants too early will create maintenance problems. This is why Phase 1 precedes Phase 2.

## Mitigations

- use one shared story spine across all decks
- sharpen each deck around one commercial promise
- build the master slide system first
- only create Phase 2 variants after the shared deck family is complete

## Acceptance criteria

This design is successful if:

1. all five Phase 1 decks feel like one coherent Beehive family
2. each offer can be understood independently in one meeting
3. TokenHub works for both technical and marketing/agency conversations
4. Training is clearly useful without overtaking the company’s core business identity
5. Phase 2 can be built from Phase 1 without rewriting the entire narrative system
