In short · answer first

Evaluate vendors by their ability to unify agentic workflows across your chosen IM platforms without lock-in. Prioritise those demonstrating robust security, seamless API integration, and a clear path to autonomous task execution. Scrutinise their architectural flexibility and real-world deployment evidence over marketing claims.

Key takeaways
  • Assess vendor lock-in risks through API and data portability
  • Demand evidence of successful multi-IM platform deployments
  • Verify security protocols meet your regional compliance mandates
  • Evaluate total cost of ownership beyond initial licensing

Define Your Integration Non-Negotiables First

Before engaging any vendor, your internal technology committee must codify the non-negotiable requirements for your agentic integration. This includes mandatory security protocols, data residency rules, and minimum performance thresholds for latency and uptime. Document these criteria in a scored evaluation matrix to ensure objective comparison during vendor demonstrations.

Crucially, define your tolerance for vendor lock-in. Specify that any proposed MCP must support standardised APIs and data export formats. This ensures you can migrate agents or switch providers without catastrophic business disruption. Your legal team should review contractual terms for data ownership and exit clauses.

Finally, establish the core use cases you will use to test vendor capabilities. These should be real-world workflows, such as automating customer onboarding or internal IT support tickets. This practical focus prevents you from being swayed by flashy demos that lack substance for your specific operational needs.

Beyond high-level principles, translate non-negotiables into a formal RFP scoring matrix. Assign weighted percentages to each criterion based on its strategic importance; for instance, security protocols might be 25%, while integration with a legacy CRM is 15%. This quantitative framework forces objective comparison and prevents vendor charm from overshadowing technical shortcomings. The procurement team, including lead architects and security officers, should collaboratively define these weights before any demos occur, ensuring the evaluation is biased toward your operational reality, not marketing promises.

Scrutinise the Architectural Approach to MCP

The Model Context Protocol is the backbone of agentic integration, but implementations vary widely. Demand a detailed architectural diagram from each vendor, specifically highlighting how their MCP server manages state, handles tool invocation, and maintains conversation history. This reveals the underlying robustness of their system.

Pay particular attention to how the vendor’s architecture handles statefulness across stateless IM platforms. A strong solution will maintain agent memory and context seamlessly as employees switch between WeCom on mobile and DingTalk on desktop. This continuity is vital for complex, multi-step tasks.

Ask vendors to explain their approach to key MCP components like the Resource Manager and SDK. Probe for their plans to adhere to emerging standards and their ability to integrate custom tools. Their answers will indicate their commitment to future-proofing and extensibility.

Request the vendor provide a detailed architecture diagram and a list of supported MCP servers. Crucially, ask for their protocol for handling a server failure or becoming unresponsive. Do they have built-in retry logic, fallback mechanisms, or circuit breakers? A robust approach should be evident in their client SDK's documentation and error handling examples. This scrutiny reveals the maturity of their implementation, separating a superficial wrapper from a deeply integrated, resilient system designed for production environments where dependencies routinely fail.

Test Cross-Platform Performance Rigorously

Vendor claims of IM-agnosticism are meaningless without proof. Insist on a structured proof-of-concept conducted on your actual technology stack. This PoC must run concurrently on at least two platforms, such as Feishu and Teams, to test the vendor’s abstraction layer under real conditions.

During the PoC, measure critical performance indicators like message delivery latency, agent response accuracy, and workflow completion rates. Observe how the system behaves during network instability or when an IM platform releases an unexpected API update. This stress-testing uncovers resilience you cannot see in slides.

Involve a pilot group of end-users from different departments to test the agent interactions. Their feedback on usability and reliability within their daily workflow is invaluable. A vendor’s solution must be judged not just by IT metrics but by its adoption and effectiveness with employees.

Performance testing must simulate real-world entropy. Beyond clean-room benchmarks, introduce variables like network latency spikes (using tools like Clumsy or tc) and concurrent user loads (via k6 or Locust) during tool calls. Measure the latency not just of the initial AI response, but the total time from user query to final action completion, including all MCP round-trips. This end-to-end latency is the true metric that impacts user experience. Establish acceptable thresholds for these degraded conditions as part of your performance non-negotiables.

Conduct a Deep Dive on Security and Compliance

In a Hong Kong context, data sovereignty is paramount. Require vendors to provide a detailed data flow map, explicitly showing where data is processed and stored at rest. Verify their infrastructure complies with local regulations and international standards like SOC 2 or ISO 27001.

Interrogate their security model for the MCP layer. How are credentials for connected tools managed? Is there robust authentication and authorisation between the agent runtime and the MCP server? Ensure their design follows a zero-trust principle, especially for integrations with your core enterprise systems.

Finally, assess their incident response and audit capabilities. They must provide comprehensive logging of all agent actions and tool usage for forensic analysis. Your compliance team needs to easily demonstrate adherence to internal policies and external regulatory requirements during an audit.

Demand evidence beyond policy documents. For compliance, require a recent SOC 2 Type II report or ISO 27001 certification, scrutinising the included carve-outs. For technical security, a penetration test report from a reputable third party is invaluable. Specifically, examine the findings related to the MCP transport layer—how are credentials for connected tools (e.g., Jira, Salesforce) secured? They should be encrypted at rest and in transit, with a clear, auditable key management strategy separate from the primary application encryption.

Evaluate the Total Cost of Ownership

Look beyond the initial licensing quote. Calculate the total cost of ownership over a three-year horizon. This must include costs for implementation, custom development, ongoing maintenance, and scaling the number of agents or users. A cheaper initial licence can mask exorbitant hidden costs.

Specifically, ask about the cost structure for scaling. Does adding a new agent incur a fixed fee, or is it based on usage? How are API calls to the MCP server billed? Understand the financial implications of your projected growth to avoid budget overruns later.

Factor in internal resource costs. A vendor with an intuitive developer toolkit and excellent documentation will reduce the time your team spends on integration and maintenance. This operational efficiency is a significant part of the long-term value equation.

A comprehensive TCO model must factor in personnel costs. Calculate the fully burdened cost of the internal developer weeks required for integration, ongoing maintenance, and building custom tools. Compare vendor management overhead: a platform with a intuitive admin console may require 0.5 FTE, while a more complex system could demand 2 FTE. Additionally, model scaling costs proactively; if user adoption increases 200%, will costs scale linearly, exponentially, or remain flat? Request the vendor provide a total-cost calculator based on your projected usage metrics.

Assess the Vendor’s Long-Term Viability

The agentic AI market is rapidly evolving. Partnering with a vendor that lacks a clear vision or financial stability poses a major risk. Investigate their funding status, revenue model, and customer retention rates. A strong roster of existing enterprise clients is a positive signal.

Review their product roadmap for the next 18 months. Does it align with your strategic direction? Are they investing in areas critical to you, such as advanced reasoning models or broader tool integrations? Their commitment to R&D indicates they will remain a competitive partner.

Gauge the quality of their support and professional services. Speak to reference customers about their experience with response times and problem resolution. The vendor’s team must act as a true partner, especially during the complex initial deployment phase.

Interrogate the vendor’s client portfolio for signals of successful, scaled deployments. Ask for two references from enterprise clients in a similar industry who have been live for over 12 months. Prepare specific questions for these references: Was the vendor’s support responsive during critical outages? How often do they deliver meaningful updates? How have they handled major API changes? The answers will reveal operational maturity and commitment to existing customers beyond the initial sale, which is critical for a long-term partnership.

Analyse the Developer and Operator Experience

The ease with which your developers can build and deploy agents is a critical success factor. Evaluate the vendor’s SDKs, CLI tools, and documentation. A well-designed developer experience accelerates time-to-value and reduces frustration, leading to higher adoption and innovation.

Equally important is the experience for IT operators who must monitor and manage the production system. Request a demo of their management dashboard. It should provide clear visibility into agent performance, error rates, and usage metrics to facilitate proactive maintenance.

Consider the learning curve for your team. Does the vendor offer comprehensive training and enablement resources? A vendor that invests in your team’s success ensures you can maximise the return on your investment and build internal expertise over time.

The true test of DX is a time-boxed proof-of-concept. Task a mid-level developer, not an expert, with building a simple custom tool (e.g., a Slack notifier) using the vendor’s SDK and documentation within a four-hour sprint. Note the hurdles: Is the local development setup straightforward? Are the API examples copy-paste usable? This exercise produces tangible artefacts—a working tool and a log of frustrations—that are more revealing than any vendor demo. High-quality DX is characterised by low friction and quick time-to-value for this exact scenario.

Make a Data-Driven Final Selection

Consolidate all your findings into a weighted scorecard. Assign scores to each evaluation criterion based on your previously defined priorities, such as security, cost, and architectural soundness. This quantitative approach removes subjectivity and facilitates a clear, defensible decision.

Schedule a final review meeting with key stakeholders from IT, security, finance, and the business units. Walk them through the scored vendor comparisons and the rationale behind the rankings. This collaborative process ensures organisational buy-in for the selected partner.

Before signing, negotiate service level agreements that directly tie to the performance metrics you measured during the PoC. These SLAs should cover uptime, support response times, and resolution timelines. A well-negotiated contract protects your investment and sets the relationship up for success.

Before finalising the selection, conduct a structured debrief with all evaluation team members. Use a pre-defined scorecard where each member scores the vendor against the weighted RFP criteria independently. Aggregate these scores to remove individual bias. The project lead should then facilitate a discussion focusing on the largest discrepancies in scoring to uncover unspoken concerns or misunderstandings. This data-driven consensus meeting ensures the final decision is transparent, defensible, and based on collective intelligence rather than the influence of a single stakeholder.
