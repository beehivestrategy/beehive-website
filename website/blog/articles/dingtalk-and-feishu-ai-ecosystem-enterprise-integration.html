The DingTalk and Feishu AI Ecosystem: A Guide for Enterprise Integration in China

BK
Beehive Strategy · June 25, 2026 · 5 min read

DingTalk and Feishu have evolved far beyond messaging apps. In 2026 they function as the central nervous system of millions of Chinese enterprises — and both platforms are building increasingly sophisticated AI ecosystems. For organisations deploying conversational BI, integration with these platforms is no longer optional; in China it is the primary deployment model. The answer to "where should our data conversations live?" is already sitting in the chat app your employees open a hundred times a day.

Key Insight: DingTalk and Feishu AI ecosystems let enterprises deploy conversational BI directly inside the IM tools employees already use, eliminating adoption barriers and delivering data insights through natural-language queries in the workflow. Alibaba has reported that DingTalk serves more than 700 million individual users across over 25 million enterprise organisations — a distribution channel no standalone analytics tool can match.

The Two Titans of Chinese Enterprise IM

China's enterprise collaboration market is dominated by two platforms, each backed by a technology giant, with WeChat Work as a powerful third force. The scale is hard to overstate: Alibaba has reported that DingTalk counts over 700 million individual users and 25 million enterprise organisations, with particularly strong penetration in manufacturing, retail, and government. Tencent has reported that WeChat Work, built on the WeChat ecosystem, serves over 100 million users across millions of businesses, making it the default for companies whose employees already live in WeChat. ByteDance's Feishu — known internationally as Lark — has grown fastest among technology companies, internet firms, and knowledge-intensive industries, where its document-first design and strong third-party ecosystem resonate.

  • DingTalk (Alibaba Group): over 700 million individual users and 25 million enterprise organisations per Alibaba; deepest penetration in manufacturing, retail, and government
  • Feishu / Lark (ByteDance): fastest-growing among technology, internet, and professional-services firms; document-first design and strong integration ecosystem
  • WeChat Work (Tencent): over 100 million users per Tencent; the natural home for companies that run on WeChat and want enterprise-grade collaboration on top

For conversational BI, this distribution is the entire game. Standalone analytics tools struggle to reach frontline users because opening another application is a barrier; an answer delivered inside a chat thread is not. The platform that wins the enterprise IM race is the one where your data questions get answered — which is why conversational BI deployments in China are now designed around these platforms from day one.

DingTalk's AI Assistant Ecosystem

DingTalk has invested heavily in AI through several coordinated initiatives. Its AI Assistant Platform, launched in 2023 and expanded through 2026, lets enterprises build custom AI assistants that operate inside DingTalk chat threads, supporting Alibaba's Tongyi Qianwen (Qwen) large language models as well as third-party models. The Enterprise Agent Market is a marketplace where organisations discover, deploy, and share pre-built AI agents — thousands of them by mid-2026, covering HR, finance, operations, and analytics functions. Underneath sits an action-based architecture: the assistant framework supports discrete API calls that the AI triggers in response to user requests, structurally similar to the Model Context Protocol's tool-calling paradigm.

That action-based design is what makes DingTalk a natural fit for MCP-powered conversational BI. A user asks a question in a group chat; the assistant decides which data tools to call; the call executes against enterprise systems; the answer returns as a rich card in the thread. The pieces are already in place — the enterprise just needs to connect its semantic layer to them. For manufacturing and retail organisations where DingTalk is the operating system of record, this turns the daily production meeting into a live data conversation.

Feishu's AI Capabilities

Feishu has taken a different but equally powerful approach. Its Intelligent Partner framework — the evolution of Feishu Bot 2.0 — supports multi-turn conversations, context awareness across chat threads, and external API invocation, with support for ByteDance's Doubao model and other domestic and international LLMs. Feishu AnyCross is an integration platform connecting Feishu to more than 300 enterprise applications, providing pre-built connectors for ERP, CRM, and database systems. And Feishu's document strength extends to AI: document analysis, automated report generation, and data extraction from spreadsheets pair naturally with conversational BI for ad-hoc analysis.

  • Intelligent Partner (Bot 2.0): multi-turn, context-aware conversations that can invoke external APIs — the hosting surface for a conversational BI assistant
  • Feishu AnyCross: 300+ enterprise application connectors, complementing MCP's data-source connectivity
  • Document AI: analysis, report generation, and spreadsheet extraction that turn ad-hoc questions into working analyses inside the flow of work

MCP Compatibility with Chinese IM Platforms

The Model Context Protocol is fundamentally a client-server protocol defining how AI models access data and tools. MCP's adoption has been extraordinary: open-sourced by Anthropic in late 2024, it was adopted by OpenAI and Google within months, and MCP SDKs now see tens of millions of downloads a month — one ecosystem tracker put the figure at roughly 97 million monthly downloads in 2026. Integrating MCP with DingTalk or Feishu requires a bridge layer: an adapter that translates between the IM platform's bot framework and the MCP servers holding enterprise data. The flow is straightforward:

  1. IM bot receives a user query in a DingTalk group chat or Feishu thread
  2. The bridge adapter receives the message through the IM platform's webhook or API
  3. The LLM processes the query and determines which MCP servers to call for the relevant data
  4. MCP servers execute against enterprise data sources — MySQL, Snowflake, SAP, warehouses — and return structured results
  5. The LLM formats the response — charts, tables, or narrative summaries — and the bridge posts it back to the conversation

Both DingTalk and Feishu support rich message formats — cards, interactive elements, chart embeds — which means conversational BI responses can include visually formatted data visualisations, not just text. The bridge pattern also keeps the enterprise in control: the IM bot receives only pre-formatted results, while the semantic layer enforces who may see what, and every query is logged for audit.

Which IM Platform Should You Build Your BI On?

Choose the platform your organisation already standardises on — adoption is the hardest part of analytics, and forcing a second communication system for data is a non-starter. Manufacturing and retail firms with deep DingTalk penetration should build on DingTalk; technology and knowledge companies already on Feishu should build there; enterprises running on WeChat Work should integrate with WeChat Work. If your organisation spans multiple platforms, the recommended architecture is a unified backend with platform-specific adapters, so the same semantic layer and governance serve every channel. IDC forecasts that China's AI spending will exceed $38 billion by 2027, and the enterprises that capture that investment will be the ones whose AI assistants meet users where they already work — in the chat apps that run their business.

Platform Comparison for BI Deployment

  • Enterprise user base: DingTalk 25M+ organisations; Feishu / Lark multiple millions of organisations and growing fastest among tech firms
  • Primary sectors: DingTalk — manufacturing, retail, government; Feishu — technology, internet, professional services
  • Default LLM: DingTalk — Qwen (Tongyi Qianwen); Feishu — Doubao (ByteDance)
  • Third-party LLM support: both support other domestic and international models via API
  • Bot framework: DingTalk — AI Assistant Platform; Feishu — Intelligent Partner (Bot 2.0)
  • Rich message cards: both support cards, interactive elements, and chart embeds
  • Enterprise app market: DingTalk Open Platform versus Feishu App Store
  • MCP integration: both via a bridge adapter connecting the bot framework to MCP servers

Practical Implementation Steps

Deploying conversational BI through DingTalk or Feishu is a well-trodden path:

  1. Choose your IM platform. Standardise on the one your organisation already uses; if you span several, plan a unified backend with platform-specific adapters
  2. Register an enterprise bot. Create a bot application through the DingTalk Open Platform or Feishu Developer Console, configuring webhook endpoints and permission scopes
  3. Deploy the MCP bridge. Connect the IM bot to your MCP server infrastructure — message routing, authentication, and response formatting
  4. Configure data connectors. Set up MCP servers for your key data sources — ERP, CRM, financial systems, warehouses
  5. Test and iterate. Start with a pilot group of 10-20 power users, collect feedback on query accuracy, response speed, and experience, then scale
