In short · answer first

The first 90 days in China are for building foundational intelligence, not generating revenue. Your goal is to validate your market thesis through direct observation, establish critical local partnerships, and deploy a minimal digital footprint to gather first-party data. This initial quarter is a diagnostic phase to pressure-test your assumptions and adapt your core strategy before committing significant investment.

Key takeaways
  • Establish a temporary operational base to facilitate direct market sensing and partner meetings
  • Prioritise securing a local ICP licence and setting up a basic Chinese website with a domestic CDN
  • Conduct a technical SEO audit specifically for Baidu's crawler and indexing requirements
  • Launch a pilot paid media campaign on WeChat to test audience targeting and creative resonance

Pre-Arrival: Assembling Your Discovery Toolkit

Before your team lands, equip them with the essential digital tools for effective discovery. Procure enterprise-grade VPN solutions for reliable access to global resources and set up local Chinese mobile numbers for all key personnel. These numbers are mandatory for registering accounts on platforms like WeChat Work, DingTalk, and key Baidu services, which are the bedrock of daily business communication and analysis.

Simultaneously, your analytics team must configure a separate Google Analytics 4 property filtered for Mainland China traffic. More critically, begin the process of applying for Baidu Tongji access. This parallel tracking is non-negotiable; it provides the comparative data set needed to understand the stark differences in user behaviour and traffic quality between domestic and international channels from day one.

Finally, compile a detailed dossier of preliminary research. This includes a list of target enterprises for partnership discussions, a preliminary keyword map translated and adjusted for Chinese search intent, and a regulatory checklist specific to your sector. This preparatory work ensures your first weeks are spent on validation and refinement, not basic information gathering.

Weeks 1-2: Ground Truth and Cultural Acclimatisation

The initial fortnight must be dedicated to immersive ground truthing. Your team’s primary objective is to move beyond desk research and experience the market directly. This means visiting retail environments, attending relevant industry meetups, and conducting informal ‘coffee meetings’ with potential partners. The goal is to sense-check your core value proposition and identify unarticulated market needs.

Parallel to this, the technical lead must initiate the application for an ICP licence. This process can be protracted, and filing the paperwork immediately is crucial. Engage a local legal consultant to review all documentation, ensuring your entity structure and business scope align with the latest regulatory requirements. This early focus mitigates a major operational roadblock later.

Simultaneously, begin the setup of your local digital presence. Secure your brand’s Chinese name on WeChat Official Accounts and key news platforms. Hire a local freelance videographer to create authentic content—such as team introductions and market first impressions—for your social channels. This builds a foundation of local credibility before any formal marketing launch.

Weeks 3-4: Technical SEO and Infrastructure Setup

With initial market sensing underway, focus shifts to technical foundations. Your development team must procure and configure a Mainland China content delivery network (CDN) service from a provider like Alibaba Cloud or Tencent Cloud. Hosting your website infrastructure domestically is imperative for achieving acceptable load speeds and indexing favourably on Baidu.

Next, conduct a comprehensive technical SEO audit specifically for Baidu’s spider. This audit must verify that your site’s code is fully crawlable, that you have submitted a sitemap to Baidu Webmaster Tools, and that all metadata is correctly translated. Pay particular attention to ensuring the site is mobile-first, as the vast majority of Chinese traffic originates from smartphones.

This phase also includes setting up your Baidu Tongji analytics account and linking it to your Webmaster Tools. Begin tracking baseline metrics for page load time, crawl errors, and initial organic traffic patterns. This data provides the first real performance indicators of your technical setup and highlights immediate areas for improvement.

Month 2: Launching Your Initial Discovery Campaigns

Month two is for launching your first targeted digital campaigns to gather quantitative data. Begin with a limited pilot campaign on WeChat Moments Advertising. Allocate a modest budget to test two to three different audience segments and multiple creative approaches. The key performance indicator here is not direct ROI but engagement rate and cost per lead for this initial learning exercise.

In parallel, launch a Zhihu (Quora-like platform) account for your sector. Publish detailed, high-quality answers to questions relevant to your product or service. This platform is highly favoured by Baidu’s algorithm for its authoritative content and can be a significant source of early organic visibility and brand establishment within knowledgeable communities.

Use the data from these initial campaigns to refine your audience personas and keyword strategy. You will likely discover that the Chinese terminology for your product category or its benefits differs significantly from your initial translations. This is a valuable outcome—it allows you to adapt your messaging before a full-scale market launch.

Weeks 7-8: Deep Dive into Baidu’s SERP Landscape

By now, you will have collected enough initial data to analyse Baidu’s SERPs in depth. This goes beyond keywords to understanding the entire content ecosystem. Task your analysts with reverse-engineering the top five results for your core seed keywords. Identify what content formats rank best—be it Baidu百科, Baidu知道, official news sites, or video content.

This analysis will reveal the necessary authority signals for your domain. It may indicate a need to pursue backlinks from specific Chinese news portals (like Sina or Sohu) or to create structured content assets that qualify for inclusion in Baidu’s own knowledge products. Allocate resources based on these concrete competitive insights.

Update your content roadmap based on these findings. If video is a dominant SERP feature, plan a series of tutorials for Youku. If问答 platforms are key, allocate resources to a sustained Zhihu engagement strategy. This ensures your content production is strategically aligned with what actually ranks, maximising the impact of your efforts.

Final Month: Validating Channels and Partner Vetting

The final month of the quarter is dedicated to validation and relationship building. Use the data from your pilot campaigns to make a go/no-go decision on each marketing channel. Double down on the one or two channels showing the most promising engagement and cost metrics, and pause those that underperform. This disciplined approach prevents budget dilution.

Concurrently, shift from exploratory partner meetings to serious vetting of two or three potential distribution or agency partners. Develop a scorecard with clear criteria: technical capability, client roster, cultural fit, and transparency in reporting. Conduct formal proposals and reference checks to shortlist a primary candidate for engagement in the next quarter.

Begin consolidating all your findings—market, technical, channel, and partner—into a revised full-year business and marketing plan. This document should outline a phased investment strategy, clear KPIs for the next quarter, and a risk register based on the real-world obstacles identified during your 90-day discovery period.

The 90-Day GEO Maturity Assessment

As the quarter concludes, conduct a formal maturity assessment against four key GEO pillars: Regulatory Compliance, Technical Infrastructure, Market Intelligence, and Partner Readiness. Rate your readiness in each area on a scale of one to five. This assessment provides a stark, objective view of your preparedness for a scaled operation and identifies critical gaps.

For example, a score of ‘one’ in Technical Infrastructure indicates your website is still hosted overseas with poor Baidu indexing, while a ‘five’ means it is fully hosted domestically with optimised performance and no crawl errors. This scoring forces an honest conversation about resource allocation for the next quarter.

This assessment becomes the foundational document for your executive steering committee. It translates three months of qualitative and quantitative learning into a clear strategic roadmap, justifying either a green light for further investment or a pause for additional strategy refinement.

Artefacts to Deliver Before Day 90

The success of this initial phase is measured by the delivery of concrete artefacts, not vague reports. The primary deliverable is a validated China Market Entry Playbook. This living document details your adapted messaging framework, approved partner shortlist, optimised channel strategy, and a detailed compliance checklist for the next stage of operations.

Second, deliver a complete Baidu SEO Performance Baseline report. This includes three months of crawl error logs, page speed metrics from within China, initial keyword ranking tracking, and a analysis of SERP features for your core terms. This provides a benchmark against which all future SEO performance will be measured.

Finally, produce a Financial Model v2.0. This model should incorporate your real-world findings on customer acquisition cost, partner fees, and operational expenses. It moves beyond theoretical assumptions to a data-informed projection, providing finance stakeholders with a credible basis for approving the next tranche of investment.
