Month Ahead: Enterprise AI Events and Announcements to Watch

By Beehive Strategy · February 1, 2026 · 6 min read

February 2026 features a dense calendar of enterprise AI events, conferences, and regulatory milestones across Asia-Pacific and globally. For enterprise technology leaders, these events represent opportunities to track industry developments, evaluate vendor capabilities, and prepare for regulatory changes that will affect AI deployment strategies.

Key Insight: Key February 2026 events include MWC Barcelona (AI in telecommunications), Singapore AI Summit (regional AI governance), and China's expected Q2 AI regulation updates. Enterprise leaders attending events with clear evaluation frameworks report 60% more actionable insights than those attending without structured objectives.

Major Conferences and Summits

Mobile World Congress (MWC) Barcelona, February 23-26, is the largest technology conference in Q1 2026 and features a significant enterprise AI track. Key themes for enterprise attendees include edge AI for telecommunications (how telcos are deploying AI at the network edge for real-time optimisation), AI-powered customer experience (conversational AI platforms for customer service at scale), and enterprise 5G-AI convergence (how 5G connectivity enables new AI deployment patterns for field operations and IoT). MWC is particularly valuable for enterprises with large field operations, retail footprints, or manufacturing facilities where edge AI and 5G create new possibilities for real-time operational intelligence.

The Singapore AI Summit, February 10-11, focuses on AI governance and cross-border data frameworks in the ASEAN region. This event is critical for enterprises operating across Southeast Asian markets, where the regulatory landscape for AI is evolving rapidly and varies significantly between countries. Key topics include ASEAN's AI governance framework (expected to be finalised in Q2 2026), cross-border data transfer mechanisms under the ASEAN Digital Data Governance Framework, and sector-specific AI regulation developments in financial services, healthcare, and manufacturing.

In China, February brings the expected release of updated AI regulation guidance from the Cyberspace Administration of China (CAC), focusing on generative AI deployment requirements and algorithmic transparency standards. While not a public conference, this regulatory milestone will significantly affect enterprises deploying AI in China and should be monitored closely. Additionally, the China Industrial Internet Summit (February 18-19 in Shenzhen) will showcase industrial AI applications in manufacturing, supply chain, and energy — with a strong emphasis on domestic technology platforms and practical deployment case studies from Chinese manufacturers.

Regulatory Milestones to Watch

February 2026 includes several regulatory milestones that enterprise AI leaders should track. The EU AI Act's first enforcement actions are expected to be announced, providing practical guidance on how the regulation is being interpreted and enforced. These enforcement actions will set precedents that affect enterprise AI deployment strategies globally, not just in the EU. The European Commission is also expected to release implementing guidelines for high-risk AI systems under the Act, which will affect any enterprise deploying AI in credit scoring, insurance pricing, employment decisions, or critical infrastructure management.

In Asia-Pacific, Singapore's Monetary Authority is expected to issue updated AI governance guidelines for financial institutions, building on the FEAT (Fairness, Ethics, Accountability, Transparency) principles released in 2024. Japan's Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry is expected to publish updated AI safety guidelines for industrial applications, particularly relevant for manufacturers with operations in Japan. South Korea's Personal Information Protection Commission is expected to release guidance on AI and personal data processing under the revised PIPA, which took effect in late 2025.

For enterprises with global AI deployments, February's regulatory developments underscore the importance of building flexible, governance-aware AI architectures. MCP-based architectures that embed governance at the data access layer are well-positioned to adapt to evolving regulatory requirements without requiring fundamental architectural changes. The semantic layer ensures consistent metric definitions that can be updated as regulatory definitions change, and the conversational BI interface makes governance status accessible to non-technical stakeholders who need to monitor compliance.

Making Events Actionable

The most common mistake in attending enterprise AI events is treating them as information-gathering exercises rather than decision-making opportunities. Enterprise leaders should attend events with a specific evaluation framework and clear decision criteria. For vendor evaluation, this means knowing which AI capabilities you need to assess, what integration requirements exist, and what governance capabilities are non-negotiable. For regulatory preparation, this means understanding which upcoming regulations affect your specific AI use cases and what architectural changes may be required for compliance.

Before attending any event, create a structured evaluation template. For conferences with vendor exhibitions, the template should include: data integration approach (does the vendor use MCP or similar standardised protocols?), semantic layer capability (how does the vendor handle business metric definitions?), governance features (access controls, audit logging, lineage tracking), deployment options (SaaS, private cloud, on-premise), IM-native delivery (WeChat Work, DingTalk, Feishu, Teams), and production reference customers in your industry. For regulatory events, the template should include: which of your current or planned AI use cases are affected, what compliance gaps exist in your current architecture, and what timeline adjustments may be needed for your AI deployment roadmap.

After each event, conduct a structured debrief within one week. Document specific, actionable insights (not general impressions), assign owners for follow-up actions, and integrate findings into your AI strategy and vendor evaluation process. Organisations that follow this structured approach to event attendance report 60% more actionable insights and 3x faster follow-up on event-identified opportunities compared to those that attend events without structured objectives and debrief processes.

February Planning Checklist

Enterprise AI leaders should complete several planning activities in February 2026. Review and update the AI deployment roadmap against regulatory developments expected in Q2 2026. Evaluate vendors encountered at MWC and regional events against your structured evaluation framework. Assess your current AI governance posture against the WEF AI Governance Maturity Model released at Davos 2026. Identify two to three specific AI use cases for pilot deployment in Q2 2026, prioritising use cases where the data infrastructure (MCP connectors, semantic layer) is already in place. Schedule a mid-year AI strategy review to assess progress against annual objectives and adjust priorities based on the rapidly evolving technology and regulatory landscape.
