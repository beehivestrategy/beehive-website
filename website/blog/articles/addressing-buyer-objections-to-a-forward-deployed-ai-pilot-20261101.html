A two-week pilot is not a rushed proof-of-concept but a structured diagnostic. It is designed to validate a specific high-value use case with your data on your infrastructure, delivering a production-ready asset and a clear go/no-go business case for the managed service.

Key takeaways
  • A pilot is a diagnostic tool to validate technical and commercial feasibility.
  • The final deliverable is a production-ready asset, not a theoretical report.
  • Clear decision rules are established upfront to determine the next step.
  • The managed service model transfers operational risk from your team to ours.

The Core Misconception: A Pilot Is Merely a Proof-of-Concept

The most frequent objection we hear is that two weeks is insufficient for meaningful AI work. Buyers envision a superficial demonstration, a flashy prototype that works in a sandbox but fails in the real world. This perception frames the pilot as a cost centre, a risky gamble on unproven technology with no guaranteed outcome, making the initial investment difficult to justify.

We reframe the pilot not as a proof-of-concept but as a targeted diagnostic. Its primary goal is to answer a single, critical question: can we reliably automate this specific business process with your data? This is a test of feasibility and integration, not of the underlying AI models, which are already proven. The value is in de-risking the larger project.

The output is a tangible, production-ready asset, such as an API endpoint or a workflow integration, not a slide deck. This asset becomes the foundation for the managed service, immediately providing value. The pilot's success is measured against predefined technical and business metrics agreed upon before a single line of code is written.

De-risking the Investment with a Structured Approach

Financial risk is mitigated through the pilot's fixed scope and cost. You are investing in a discrete, time-boxed experiment with a binary outcome. The managed service proposal, presented at the pilot's conclusion, is based on empirical evidence from your own environment, not market speculation. This eliminates the fear of a runaway project with endless scope creep.

Technical risk is addressed by working directly inside your cloud tenancy or on-premises infrastructure from day one. We confront data access, governance, and integration challenges immediately. This avoids the common pitfall of a successful demo that later collapses under the weight of real-world data latency or security protocols.

Operational risk is transferred to us. During the managed service phase, we assume responsibility for the model's performance, monitoring, and updates. Your team gains a capability without the burden of recruiting rare AI talent or building new operational procedures. Our service level agreements guarantee uptime and model accuracy.

The Pre-Pilot Scoping: Defining the Battlefield

Success is predetermined in a one-week preparation phase. Our commercial lead and your procurement team agree on a master service agreement. Simultaneously, our technical lead works with your data engineering and business unit heads to define the precise use case and success criteria. This alignment is the most critical step.

We establish three concrete artefacts. First, a joint responsibility matrix (RACI) outlining data access, stakeholder availability, and approval workflows. Second, a definition of 'done' document listing the exact technical deliverables. Third, a business case template that will be populated with pilot results to justify—or not—the managed service.

A kick-off workshop finalises these plans. Key attendees include your lead data steward, the head of the business unit that owns the process, and an IT security representative. Their collective sign-off on the scope ensures organisational buy-in and prevents blockers during the intensive two-week delivery sprint.

The pre-pilot scoping culminates in a formal 'Pilot Charter' document, co-signed by both parties. This charter explicitly lists the three to five key performance indicators (KPIs) that will define success, such as 'reduce manual data entry errors by 40%' or 'automate 70% of customer inquiry categorisation'. Crucially, it also details the specific data assets required, their owners within the client's organisation, and the secure data transfer protocol. This document acts as the project's constitution, preventing scope creep and ensuring all stakeholders are aligned on the measurable outcomes before a single line of code is written.

A critical artefact developed during scoping is the 'Stakeholder Map and Communication Plan'. This identifies not only the primary business sponsor and end-users but also ancillary teams like IT security, legal, and compliance. For each group, the map defines their role (e.g., 'data provider', 'approver', 'end-user'), their primary concerns (e.g., data privacy, integration overhead), and the agreed-upon channels for updates. This proactive step ensures smooth logistical coordination and mitigates the risk of a critical team being unaware of or blocking the pilot's progress.

A Day-by-Day Look Inside the Two-Week Pilot

Days one to three are dedicated to environment setup and data ingestion. Our engineers, granted carefully scoped access, work to connect to your designated data sources. The focus is on building a robust data pipeline that can be monitored and maintained long-term, not on extracting a one-off sample for a demo.

The core development work occurs on days four through ten. This involves training a model on your proprietary data, refining its logic, and integrating it into a mock version of your production system, such as a CRM or ERP. Your team reviews intermediate results daily in stand-up meetings, providing immediate feedback.

The final days are for validation and packaging. We conduct rigorous testing against the predefined success metrics and load-test the API endpoint. The final handover includes the model itself, full documentation, a performance report, and the completed business case for the managed service expansion.

Days 1-3 are dedicated to intensive data onboarding and environment configuration. Our engineers work alongside your IT lead to establish a secure, isolated sandbox environment. The focus is on ingesting the pre-agreed datasets, which often requires writing custom connectors or ETL scripts. Concurrently, the product owner conducts detailed user interviews with the identified frontline staff to map their exact workflows and pain points. This ensures the AI model isn't built in a vacuum but is designed to slot seamlessly into existing operational procedures, maximising adoption and tangible impact.

The final two days of the pilot are reserved for user acceptance testing (UAT) and the creation of the final business case. A select group of end-users is given access to the tool to perform their daily tasks, providing immediate feedback on usability and value. Meanwhile, our analysts compile the fortnight's performance data against the pre-defined KPIs into a dashboard. This isn't just raw accuracy scores; it translates model performance into business metrics, such as time saved per employee per day or the potential reduction in operational overhead, providing the concrete figures needed for the go/no-go decision.

The Deliverables: More Than a Working Model

The primary deliverable is a production-ready software asset. This is typically a containerised API or a module that plugs directly into your existing business intelligence platform. It is delivered with a full bill of materials and is yours to keep, regardless of your decision on the managed service.

A second key artefact is the investment case. This document quantifies the pilot's results, projecting the full-scale operational and financial benefits of the managed service. It provides the concrete data your finance department requires, detailing the expected return on investment and total cost of ownership.

Finally, you receive a strategic roadmap. This outlines the three to five subsequent high-value use cases identified during the pilot, presenting a clear vision for scaling AI across the organisation. It turns a single experiment into a long-term digital transformation strategy.

Beyond the model itself, you receive a comprehensive 'Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) Forecast'. This detailed breakdown estimates the ongoing operational costs for running the AI at scale, including cloud computing resources, potential licensing fees, and the estimated FTE effort required from your team for maintenance and monitoring. This transparency allows for accurate budgeting and prevents unexpected financial surprises post-deployment, turning a technical prototype into a clear financial proposition.

A key deliverable is the 'Integration Roadmap'. This technical document outlines the specific APIs, data pipelines, and architecture changes required to move the pilot from the sandbox environment into full production integration with your core systems (e.g., CRM, ERP). It provides high-level effort estimates for your technical teams, clearly separating tasks to be handled by our engineers from those your IT department would need to own. This roadmap de-risks the scaling process by providing a clear, actionable plan for the engineering work ahead.

The Clear Go/No-Go Decision at Pilot Completion

The decision to proceed is not subjective. It is governed by rules established during scoping. A technical threshold, such as 95% model accuracy on your validation data set, must be met. A business threshold, such as projecting a 30% reduction in manual effort, must also be achieved.

If both thresholds are met, the transition to a managed service is seamless. We present a statement of work based on the actual resource requirements observed during the pilot. The model is already running in your environment; the managed service simply activates ongoing monitoring, refinement, and support.

If the pilot fails to meet the thresholds, it is still a success. You have avoided a costly investment in an unfeasible project and gained invaluable insights into your data readiness. The investment is capped at the pilot cost, and we often identify adjacent opportunities worth exploring.

The decision to proceed is governed by a pre-agreed-upon scoring rubric applied to the pilot's results. This rubric assigns weighted scores to each KPI (e.g., accuracy score weighted at 40%, user adoption rate at 30%, TCO forecast at 30%). A pilot must achieve a minimum composite score of 80% to be considered a 'Go'. For example, even a highly accurate model would fail if the user adoption metrics are catastrophically low, forcing a re-evaluation of the UI or the underlying business process instead of a full-scale deployment.

Contrasting Our Model with Traditional Consultancies

Traditional firms often operate on a strategy-and-report model. They conduct a lengthy discovery, produce a hefty report recommending AI, and then depart. You are left with a theoretical plan and the daunting task of execution, often requiring a separate and expensive procurement process for another vendor.

Our forward-deployed model inverts this. We start by doing. The pilot is execution itself, compressing discovery and initial build into one action-oriented engagement. We prove value through action, not analysis. This is only possible because our teams are engineers, not just strategists, who are authorised to build on the spot.

The managed service continuation is a natural evolution, not a new project. There is no re-procurement or second sales cycle. The commercial and technical frameworks are already in place, allowing us to scale the solution immediately based on the trust and evidence established during the pilot.

Preparing Your Organisation for the Pilot Engagement

Identify the right internal champion. This should be a business lead whose key performance indicators will be directly impacted by the automation, such as a head of customer operations or a chief financial officer. Their sponsorship is crucial for driving internal cooperation and adoption.

Assemble a micro-team for the two weeks. Assign a technical liaison from your data or IT team for access provisioning. Assign a subject-matter expert from the business unit to provide domain context and validate outputs. Their dedicated time is the single biggest predictor of pilot success.

Pre-allocate the data sets. Work with your data governance team beforehand to identify and secure access to the specific, anonymised data required for the use case. Overcoming data access hurdles before day one prevents the pilot from stalling and ensures we focus on engineering, not administration.

Appoint a single, empowered 'Pilot Captain' from your side. This individual, often from operations or a dedicated transformation office, is responsible for unblocking issues, coordinating internal resources, and providing rapid decisions. Their primary tool is the pre-defined stakeholder map. They pre-schedule brief daily syncs with our team and a weekly steering committee meeting with leadership to report progress. This ensures internal bottlenecks—like gaining access to a new data source—are resolved within hours, not days, maintaining the pilot's aggressive momentum.

Prepare your data custodians and subject matter experts (SMEs) for their roles. The IT team should be ready to provision a secure sandbox environment and grant necessary data access on day one. The frontline SMEs must be briefed that their participation in user interviews and testing is critical and should be prioritised. Having these individuals identified and their time protected in advance is the single biggest factor in ensuring the pilot runs smoothly and collects the high-quality feedback needed to build a truly effective tool.
