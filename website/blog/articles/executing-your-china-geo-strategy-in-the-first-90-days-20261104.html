In short · answer first

The first 90 days in China are for validation and agile learning, not full-scale rollout. Your primary goal is to pressure-test your market entry thesis with real users, establish foundational localised SEO assets, and build the operational cadence for data-driven iteration. Success is measured by the quality of insights gathered, not immediate revenue.

Key takeaways
  • Secure your .cn domain and ICP licence immediately to avoid delays
  • Build a minimum viable content hub focused on solving local problems
  • Establish a weekly cadence for reviewing Baidu search console data
  • Validate one channel assumption before scaling any marketing spend

Pre-Launch: Securing Your Digital Footprint

Before your first day, ensure your legal entity is established and can apply for an ICP licence. This is the mandatory permit for any commercial website hosted in mainland China. Without it, your site will be blocked. Engage a local hosting provider like Alibaba Cloud or Tencent Cloud to expedite the process, as they understand the documentation requirements and can often liaise directly with the MIIT.

Simultaneously, begin the process of registering your .cn country code top-level domain (ccTLD). While .com domains can be used, a .cn domain signals a long-term commitment to the Chinese market and is favoured by local search engines. Domain registration requires business documentation, so your legal team must be prepared. This process can take several weeks, so initiate it on day one.

Parallel to this, conduct a technical SEO audit specifically for the Chinese ecosystem. This means testing site speed from within the Great Firewall, ensuring all fonts render correctly for Simplified Chinese characters, and verifying that your site is crawlable by Baidu’s Spider. Tools like Baidu Webmaster Tools are essential for this diagnostic phase.

Weeks 1-2: Establishing Your Listening Posts

Your initial focus must be on social listening and search analysis, not broadcasting. Deploy a team to monitor key platforms like Weibo, Xiaohongshu, and Zhihu for conversations related to your industry, competitors, and core value propositions. The goal is to understand the language your buyers use, their common pain points, and the influencers they trust.

Complement this qualitative listening with quantitative data from Baidu Keyword Planner and 5118.com. Map the search volume and difficulty for your core terminology, but pay greater attention to the long-tail, question-based queries that reveal user intent. This data will form the bedrock of your content strategy and help you avoid translating irrelevant Western keywords.

Synthesise these findings into a initial market sentiment brief. This artefact should detail the top three observed customer frustrations, the language used to describe them, and a list of emerging topics not being addressed by incumbent players. This brief guides your first content creation sprint and is your first major deliverable.

Assign team members to specific listening post roles: a Baidu Index analyst to track keyword search volume trends, a WeChat Explorer to monitor trending articles and mini-program launches within your sector, and a dedicated social sentiment tracker for platforms like Douban and Zhihu. Their first deliverable should be a weekly Listening Post Digest, a 3-5 page report highlighting significant shifts in consumer discussion topics, emerging competitor campaigns, and potential regulatory mentions. This artefact ensures that qualitative observations are systematically documented and shared across the entire cross-border team, turning raw data into an actionable intelligence feed.

Weeks 3-4: The Minimum Viable Content Hub

Resist the urge to translate your global website. Instead, build a small, dedicated Chinese microsite or section with 5-10 foundational pieces of content designed explicitly for the insights gathered in your listening phase. Each piece should be a deep dive answering a specific, high-intent question discovered on Zhihu or Baidu Knows.

Content must be text-based and SEO-optimised for Baidu’s algorithm, which heavily weights text relevance and freshness. Avoid heavy JavaScript elements and ensure pages load under three seconds on Chinese mobile networks. Each article should be properly interlinked and include meta descriptions tailored to encourage click-throughs from Baidu’s search results pages.

This hub is your testing ground. Its performance—measured by organic traffic, time on page, and engagement rates—will tell you if you are solving the right problems. The hub should be managed in a CMS that allows for rapid iteration and A/B testing of headlines and content angles based on weekly data reviews.

The MVP hub must include three core content types: a 'Hero' pillar page (e.g., 'The Ultimate Guide to [Your Product Category] in China'), 2-3 'Hub' articles addressing specific user pain points with long-tail keywords, and 4-5 'Help' pieces answering straightforward FAQ-style questions. For example, a skincare brand’s hub would feature a hero guide on 'Building a Mandarin Skincare Routine', hub articles on 'Treating Hormonal Acne in Your 30s', and help content like 'Is Vitamin C Serum Safe for Sensitive Skin?'. This structure ensures you cast a wide net for search traffic while building topical authority.

Weeks 5-6: Initial Activation and Channel Testing

With content live, begin low-volume activation to drive initial traffic. This is not a broad brand campaign. It is a targeted effort to bring your identified audience segments to your hub. For B2B, this may mean sponsoring a specific answer on Zhihu; for B2C, collaborating with a niche Key Opinion Consumer on Xiaohongshu for a product deep-dive.

Each activation must be meticulously tracked. Use UTM parameters for all links and monitor the flow of traffic through your hub. The goal is to identify which channel and which content angle drives the most qualified visitors. Allocate a small test budget to run paid search ads on Baidu for your top three keywords to gauge click-through rates and cost-per-click.

Hold a weekly growth meeting with all stakeholders to review the channel performance data. The decision rule is simple: double down on the channel showing the lowest cost per engaged session and pause underperforming tactics. This meeting produces a weekly channel performance scorecard, your key governance artefact.

Weeks 7-8: Data Synthesis and Strategy Refinement

By now, you will have accumulated enough user behaviour data from Baidu Analytics and social platforms to make informed decisions. The primary task is to analyse the user journey from search query to content engagement. Identify which pieces are ranking, which are generating leads, and where users are dropping off.

This analysis will likely reveal gaps. A high-performing article on a specific problem may indicate demand for a related product feature not highlighted on your site. A common search query with no good answer in your hub represents a clear content gap to be filled in the next sprint. Update your keyword map and content backlog accordingly.

Formalise these insights into a revised GEO strategy document. This version should be grounded in local data, not assumptions. It must outline the validated content pillars, the proven acquisition channels for initial traction, and a roadmap for the next quarter based on what you now know works.

Establish a clear decision rule for scaling: any channel or content format that achieves a Cost-Per-Lead (CPL) 30% below the initial target benchmark and generates a minimum of 50 qualified leads in the two-week period should be prioritized for increased budget allocation in Q2. Conversely, immediately pause campaigns that exceed the target CPL by more than 50% with no lead conversion after the first week of testing. This data-driven triage prevents emotional attachment to underperforming tactics and forces resource concentration on the highest probability of success, which is critical for justifying further investment.

Weeks 9-12: Scaling What Works and Planning Q2

The final phase of the first 90 days is about building momentum. Shift resources from testing to scaling the one or two channels that have demonstrated clear efficacy. If Zhihu answers are driving high-quality leads, develop a programme to systematically address relevant questions. If a specific content format works, produce more of it.

Begin planning for the second quarter based on your validated learnings. This includes budgeting for scaled channel spending, building a content production calendar aligned with your proven topics, and considering technical SEO investments like site speed optimisation or implementing Baidu’s structured data protocols to enhance rich snippet appearances.

The final deliverable is a strategic recommendation for the leadership team. This report should clearly state what has been validated, what requires a pivot, and what the investment thesis is for the next quarter. It transforms your 90-day exploration into an executable, funded plan for growth.

Building Your Cross-Border Operational Cadence

Sustaining momentum requires a disciplined operational rhythm between your global and China teams. Institute a weekly sync focused solely on data review. The China-based marketing lead should present the weekly performance scorecard, highlighting key metrics and insights, while the global team provides context and strategic guidance.

The monthly meeting should be more strategic, reviewing progress against the 90-day goals and adjusting the key initiatives for the next month. This meeting must produce clear decisions on resource allocation and priority shifts, documented in a simple one-page memo circulated to all stakeholders.

Use a shared digital workspace, such as a Notion or Confluence page, as a single source of truth. This hub should contain all key artefacts: the keyword map, content calendar, performance dashboards, and meeting notes. This transparency prevents misalignment and ensures everyone is working from the same set of validated facts.

Measuring Success in the Validation Phase

Avoid the common mistake of measuring early success by revenue. Your key performance indicators for the first 90 days are leading indicators of market fit. The primary metric should be organic traffic growth from Baidu search, specifically for your target keyword clusters. A steady upward trend validates your content and SEO strategy.

Secondary metrics include engagement rates (time on page, pages per session) on your content hub, which signal content relevance, and the cost per acquired contact from your initial channel tests. The conversion rate of these initial contacts, even if small, provides early signal on product-market fit and sales readiness.

The ultimate measure of success is the confidence of your strategic decisions moving into quarter two. You should be able to definitively state which channels to fund, which content topics to double down on, and what messaging resonates. This clarity is the ROI of your initial 90-day investment.

Beyond lead volume, define what constitutes a 'qualified' lead for the China market. This must include firmographic data points such as the user's location within Mainland China, the company name (for B2B), and the specific resource they downloaded. The most critical success metric, however, is the percentage of leads that progress to a meaningful next step, such as attendance at a targeted WeChat webinar, activation of a free tool, or a direct inquiry via WhatsApp/WeChat. This measures market-specific engagement intensity, not just top-of-funnel interest, validating whether your messaging truly resonates.
