Build vs Buy Conversational BI: A Total-Cost-of-Ownership View

BK
Beehive Strategy · September 9, 2026 · 4 min read

The build-versus-buy decision for conversational BI is usually framed around license fees. That is the wrong unit. The real cost is the engineering and governance labor you commit to for years.

Key Statistics: Key points: building in-house hides years of semantic-layer maintenance, evaluation and security work; buying managed shifts that labor to a vendor while you keep control of definitions; for most mid-market firms, buy wins on TCO and time-to-value.

The license is the smallest number

A managed platform's fee is visible and bounded. The in-house build's cost is mostly invisible: the engineers who build the query engine, the analyst who maintains the semantic layer, the on-call who patches the security hole at 2am. Those salaries dwarf any license over three years. TCO means counting all of it — build, run, secure, govern — not just the sticker price.

What building actually buys you

In-house gives maximum control and zero vendor lock-in, in theory. In practice it buys a permanent engineering obligation: every model update, every schema change, every security advisory becomes your team's work. For a firm whose business is not software, that is a distraction from the actual product. Build only makes sense if analytics is your differentiator or you have a platform team with spare capacity — rare for mid-market firms.

What buying actually buys you

A managed platform provides the engine, the guardrails, the evaluation scaffolding and the security posture. You still own the important part: your semantic layer, your definitions, your access policy. The vendor runs the pipes; you keep the meaning. This is the often-missed point: buy does not mean surrender control. It means rent the commodity, own the moat.

The semantic layer is yours either way

Whether build or buy, you must define what 'revenue' means and govern it. That work cannot be outsourced, because it is your business logic. The decision is only about who runs the machinery around it. So do not let a vendor pitch confuse you: the definitions are always yours. Budget for that work regardless of the choice.

Time-to-value is a cost too

A build takes quarters before the first reliable answer. A managed platform, wired to your semantic layer, can answer questions in weeks. In a market where competitors are shipping AI features now, the delay itself is a competitive cost. The fastest path to value is usually: managed engine plus your own governed definitions, not a greenfield build.

The hidden risk of build: stall

Many in-house BI builds stall after the impressive demo, because the unglamorous maintenance never got staffed. The result is an expensive prototype that quietly dies. Buying converts that risk into a vendor's operational obligation. If you cannot name the owner who will maintain the build for three years, you have already chosen buy in practice.

A decision checklist

Choose build if: analytics is your product, you have platform capacity, and you need bespoke control. Choose buy if: you want reliable answers fast, you lack a data platform team, and your differentiator is your domain expertise, not your tooling. For the typical mid-market firm, the TCO and time-to-value math points firmly to managed — provided you keep ownership of definitions and access.

The bottom line

Do not mistake a low license for low cost, or a high license for lost control. Count the labor, count the years, and keep the definitions. The right answer is usually to rent the engine and own the meaning.

Key takeaways

  • The license is the smallest number; labor is the real cost.
  • Buying means rent the engine, own the moat.
  • The semantic layer is yours either way.
  • Most mid-market firms should buy on TCO and time-to-value.

Where to start

If analytics is not your product and you lack a platform team, choose managed: a vendor runs the engine and guardrails while you keep definitions and access. The fastest path to value is a managed engine plus your own governed layer.
