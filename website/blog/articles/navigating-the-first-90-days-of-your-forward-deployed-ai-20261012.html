In short · answer first

The first 90 days transform a successful pilot into a production-ready AI capability. This period focuses on operationalising the pilot's output, embedding your team into our delivery cadence, and establishing the governance for long-term value. Success is measured by a transition from project-based delivery to a stable, evolving managed service that meets your defined performance and business outcome metrics.

Key takeaways
  • Establish a joint governance council within the first two weeks to own strategic direction.
  • Define and track three key performance indicators beyond model accuracy from day one.
  • Complete a full knowledge transfer session for your in-house team by day 60.
  • Formalise a 90-day rollout plan for the initial AI capability before the pilot concludes.

The Strategic Handover from Pilot to Production

The conclusion of your two-week forward-deployed engineering pilot is not an end, but a formal handover into the managed service phase. This transition is a critical business process, not merely a technical one. Our first joint task is to convene a handover workshop involving both pilot and managed service teams. The objective is to ratify the pilot's findings, formally accept the proof-of-concept artefacts, and explicitly define the 'known unknowns' that will shape the initial 90-day roadmap for the production environment.

A core artefact from this workshop is the Joint Production Readiness Assessment. This document moves beyond technical validation to assess operational readiness across four pillars: data pipeline stability, model performance benchmarks, your internal change management preparedness, and the defined support protocols. Each pillar receives a red-amber-green rating based on mutually agreed criteria. This assessment provides the factual baseline against which all subsequent progress in the first 30 days will be measured, ensuring no assumptions are carried forward unchecked.

Finally, this handover mandates a clear definition of 'Day One' for the managed service. This is the specific date upon which our service level agreement officially commences, and your operational teams begin using the AI outputs for decision-making. Defining this date triggers a series of contractual and operational protocols, including access provisioning, monitoring dashboard activation, and the start of the weekly performance reporting cycle. Clarity here prevents ambiguity and aligns both parties on the official start of the production journey.

Weeks 1-4: Establishing the Operational Core

The initial month is dedicated to instilling operational discipline and stabilising the core system. Your assigned forward-deployed AI engineer will immediately establish three foundational routines: a daily stand-up with your nominated technical lead, a weekly performance review with business stakeholders, and a bi-weekly governance council meeting. These routines are designed for rapid feedback, ensuring any integration issues or performance drifts are identified and remediated within hours, not days, preventing small problems from escalating.

Concurrently, our team will operationalise the monitoring and alerting framework tailored to your environment. This involves deploying pre-configured dashboards that track key performance indicators, such as model accuracy drift, data pipeline latency, and business outcome metrics. Alert thresholds are calibrated during this period; for example, an alert might be triggered if prediction confidence drops below a predefined level for three consecutive cycles. These artefacts provide your team with real-time visibility into the health of the AI service from the outset.

A primary deliverable for this first month is the inaugural version of the AI Service Run Book. This living document, co-authored by our engineers and your team, details all operational procedures: how to interpret alerts, who to contact for specific issues, and the standard operating procedures for routine maintenance. This ensures knowledge is institutionalised from the start, building your internal team's confidence and capability to interact with and ultimately own the system long-term.

Defining and Tracking Early-Win Metrics

While technical stability is crucial, the managed service must demonstrate tangible business value within the first quarter. We work with you to define two to three 'early-win' metrics that are directly tied to a business outcome. These are distinct from model accuracy; for example, a metric could be 'reduction in manual processing time per transaction' or 'increase in lead conversion rate for scored prospects'. These metrics must be measurable with your existing systems to provide a clear baseline for comparison.

We establish a lightweight but rigorous measurement process for these metrics. This often involves creating a dedicated tracking dashboard that juxtaposes the AI-driven output against the previous manual process or a control group. The data is reviewed weekly in the performance meeting to analyse trends, validate the causality of the AI intervention, and identify any unintended consequences. This empirical approach moves the conversation from theoretical potential to demonstrated value, building stakeholder confidence.

If the early-win metrics are not trending positively by the end of the first 60 days, a predefined contingency process is activated. This is not a failure but a core part of the agile managed service approach. The process involves a root-cause analysis workshop to determine if the issue is data quality, model applicability, or a change in external conditions. The output is a revised hypothesis and an adjusted plan for the next 30-day sprint, ensuring the service remains aligned with business objectives.

The Joint Governance Model for Strategic Alignment

Effective governance is the mechanism that ensures the AI service evolves in lockstep with your business strategy. We formalise a Joint Governance Council that meets every two weeks. Your representation should include a business decision-maker, a technical lead, and a risk or compliance officer. Our team is represented by the delivery lead and AI strategist. This forum is empowered to make strategic decisions on the service's roadmap, prioritisation of new features, and allocation of resources.

The council's agenda is standardised around four items: review of performance against SLA, review of business outcome metrics, assessment of emerging risks or opportunities, and prioritisation of the backlog for the next development sprint. Decisions are recorded in a decision log, which is a key governance artefact. This structure prevents meetings from becoming mere status updates and ensures every session drives the service forward strategically.

A critical rule is established: any proposed change that impacts the core model, data sources, or defined ethical guidelines must be tabled at the governance council for approval. This rule prevents tactical changes from creating strategic technical debt or compliance issues. The council uses a simple decision matrix, evaluating proposals based on estimated effort, expected business impact, and potential risk, to ensure resources are always focused on the highest-value initiatives.

Knowledge Transfer and Building Internal Capability

A key objective of the first 90 days is to build your team's proficiency to eventually operate the AI service independently. Our knowledge transfer plan is not a single event but a continuous process integrated into daily operations. It is structured around three roles: your AI product owner, who learns to manage the backlog and define business requirements; your data engineer, who learns to manage the data pipelines; and your operations lead, who learns to handle first-line support and monitoring.

Transfer occurs through paired working sessions, documented run books, and weekly 'lunch and learn' workshops focused on specific components of the system. For example, a session might deconstruct the model's code, explaining the rationale for specific features and how to retrain it with new data. The success of this transfer is measured by your team's ability to independently execute predefined tasks, such as diagnosing a data pipeline failure or interpreting a model drift alert.

By day 60, we aim to conduct a table-top exercise, a simulated operational scenario where your team leads the response to a provided incident with our team in an advisory role. This exercise validates the effectiveness of the knowledge transfer, identifies any gaps in understanding or documentation, and builds confidence. The output is an updated training plan for the final 30 days, ensuring your team is prepared for increasing ownership.

Preparing for the First Model Evolution

A static model is a decaying asset. Therefore, the first 90 days must include preparations for the first planned model retraining or enhancement. This begins with establishing a robust model performance baseline and monitoring for signals of drift or degradation. We collaboratively define the triggers for retraining, which are typically a significant drop in accuracy, a shift in the underlying data distribution, or a change in business rules captured as a new requirement.

We then design and test the retraining pipeline itself. This involves versioning all code and data, automating the training process, and defining the validation gates a new model must pass before it can be considered for deployment. A key decision rule is established: any new model must be evaluated against a champion-challenger framework, running in shadow mode alongside the production model to verify its performance does not degrade real-world operations before a full cut-over.

Finally, a formal model change management procedure is socialised with your governance council. This procedure outlines the steps from trigger identification to deployment, including who approves the change, how rollback is handled if issues arise, and how the new model's performance is tracked post-deployment. Having this procedure agreed upon before it is needed ensures the first model evolution is a smooth, controlled process rather than a reactive emergency.

The Day 90 Review and Strategic Roadmap

The culmination of the first quarter is a formal Day 90 Review, a strategic session separate from the regular governance cycle. This review assesses progress against the initial goals set during the pilot handover. It answers three questions: Has the service achieved operational stability? Are we delivering the expected business value? Is the joint team operating effectively? The answers are based on data from performance reports, metric tracking, and stakeholder interviews.

The most important output of this review is the strategic roadmap for the next two quarters. This roadmap prioritises initiatives such as scaling the AI capability to new business units, integrating with additional enterprise systems, or developing new model features. Initiatives are prioritised based on a value-versus-complexity assessment, ensuring the continued alignment of the AI service with your most pressing business objectives. This moves the engagement from a stabilisation phase to a growth and innovation phase.

The review also results in a revised operating model if necessary. For instance, if your internal team has achieved a high level of proficiency, the model may shift to a lighter-touch managed service with our team providing strategic oversight rather than hands-on-keyboard support. Alternatively, if scaling is the priority, the model may be expanded. This deliberate review ensures the service relationship evolves intentionally to match your organisation's changing needs and capabilities.

Mitigating Risks in the Initial Deployment Phase

The first 90 days present unique risks that must be proactively managed. A common risk is integration fragility, where the AI service interacts unpredictably with legacy systems. Our mitigation is the implementation of circuit breakers and fall-back procedures within the first two weeks. For example, if the AI service fails, the system automatically defaults to a previous rule-based method, ensuring business continuity while the issue is diagnosed, thus protecting operational integrity from the start.

Another significant risk is stakeholder expectation misalignment, where the business perceives the service as a finished product rather than an evolving capability. We mitigate this through the transparent, metrics-driven review cycles established early on and by publishing the 90-day plan. Showcasing a plan with clear milestones manages expectations by demonstrating that value delivery is a journey with incremental, measurable steps, not a single big-bang event.

Finally, we address the risk of model underperformance in the wild. Despite rigorous pilot testing, real-world data can reveal edge cases. Our protocol mandates that the first 30 days operate in a 'high-touch' monitoring mode, with our engineers on standby to analyse any prediction errors immediately. This rapid feedback loop allows us to fine-tune the model or its pre-processing steps quickly, often before end-users even notice a problem, ensuring the service maintains its credibility.
