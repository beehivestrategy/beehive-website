Navigating the First 90 Days of Your Forward-Deployed AI Pilot

In short · answer first

The first 90 days transform a successful pilot into a scalable managed service. This period demands a structured approach to integrate the AI solution, establish robust governance, and demonstrate clear business value. A methodical transition ensures operational stability, aligns stakeholders, and builds the foundation for long-term growth and innovation.

Key takeaways
  • Establish a dedicated cross-functional transition team within the first week.
  • Define and track three key business value metrics from day one.
  • Formalise operational handover procedures by the end of month one.
  • Schedule the first strategic roadmap review before day 75.

Weeks 1-2: Securing the Pilot's Foundation

Your immediate focus post-pilot is securing the initial proof-of-concept. The first week is for a detailed handover workshop. This session, led by your pilot team and our delivery lead, codifies every artefact: the final model weights, validation results, data processing scripts, and the project repository. This creates a single source of truth, preventing knowledge loss and ensuring the operational team has everything required to run the system.

Concurrently, form your internal transition team. This group must include a business lead from the pilot’s sponsoring department, a technical owner from your IT function, and a representative from operations. Their first joint action is to ratify the pilot’s success criteria and formally accept the deliverables. This act signals the official transition from a experimental project to a business-owned asset.

Finally, establish baseline performance metrics. If the pilot measured a 15% reduction in manual processing time, confirm this figure against live data. Document the infrastructure’s current state, including compute costs and API latency. This baseline is your benchmark for all future improvements and is non-negotiable for justifying the managed service’s ongoing investment to finance stakeholders.

Establish a formal RACI matrix immediately to delineate responsibilities for data access, model validation, and deployment approvals. The Data Steward is Responsible for provisioning the initial, sanitised dataset, while the Lead AI Engineer is Accountable for its technical integrity. The Security Officer must be Consulted on all access protocols, and the Business Unit Head is Informed upon completion. This artefact prevents ambiguity and ensures critical security and compliance checks are not overlooked in the initial rush to access data and begin development.

Weeks 3-4: Operationalising the AI Workflow

The next fortnight is about embedding the AI into live business workflows. Begin by mapping the pilot’s inference process to your production environment. This involves coordinating with your infrastructure team to provision approved resources, defining access controls for the model API, and integrating outputs into downstream systems like your CRM or ERP. The goal is a seamless, secure, and scalable operational pipeline.

Simultaneously, draft the standard operating procedures (SOPs) for the managed service. These documents detail incident response protocols, such as who to contact for a model performance alert versus a system outage. They also cover routine tasks: how to retrain the model with new data, how to monitor for data drift, and the approval process for any code changes. Clarity here prevents future operational friction.

Formalise the governance model. Schedule the first monthly review meeting with all stakeholders. The agenda should review performance against the baseline, assess upcoming resource needs, and prioritise the backlog for the next development cycle. This meeting is the steering wheel for the entire programme, ensuring business needs continuously guide technical execution.

Implement a CI/CD pipeline specifically configured for model updates and A/B testing in the staging environment. For example, a new model version predicting equipment failure can be deployed to 10% of assets while the incumbent model serves the rest. A decision rule should be established: if the new model shows a 15% higher precision rate over a 72-hour period with no increase in false negatives, it is automatically promoted to full production. This automation ensures rapid, data-driven iteration without manual intervention for every minor update.

Create a mandatory 'Model Fact Sheet' for each version deployed. This artefact records training data provenance, feature definitions, performance metrics on test sets, and known limitations. For instance, a fact sheet might note that a computer vision model for damage assessment was trained predominantly on images from North Atlantic voyages and may underperform in tropical conditions. This transparency allows operational teams to understand the AI's confidence boundaries and make informed decisions.

Month 2: Proving Value and Scaling Usage

With the system operational, month two focuses on demonstrating and amplifying value. Your transition team’s primary task is to track and communicate impact. Report on the agreed business metrics, such as cost savings or processing accuracy, to the sponsoring executive. Concrete evidence of return on investment is crucial for securing budget for the next phase of expansion.

Identify and execute on the first expansion opportunity. This might be extending the model’s use to a new team within the same department or applying it to a slightly different data type. The key is choosing a low-risk, high-value increment that builds on the existing infrastructure. A successful small-scale expansion proves scalability and builds organisational confidence.

This is also the time to initiate a change management plan. Work with internal communications to announce the AI’s success and its new operational status. Offer training sessions or office hours for staff who will interact with the system. Managing this adoption process proactively mitigates resistance and accelerates the realisation of benefits across the organisation.

To prove value quantitatively, initiate a controlled comparison between the AI-assisted process and the legacy manual process. For example, track the time-to-resolution for logistics optimisation recommendations handled by the AI system versus those handled by a control group of planners using traditional methods. Calculate the net promoter score (NPS) from end-users interacting with the AI's insights. A positive delta in both efficiency and user satisfaction provides irrefutable evidence of value to present to stakeholders for budget justification for further scaling.

Define explicit scaling criteria in a checklist format. Before expanding the pilot to a second business unit or geographic region, verify: 1) API response times remain under 200ms at peak load, 2) the model's precision/recall metrics do not degrade by more than 5% on the new unit's data sample, and 3) the new unit has identified a dedicated liaison officer. This gates expansion behind performance, stability, and organisational readiness, preventing premature scaling that could undermine the pilot's proven success.

Month 3: Strategic Handover and Future Roadmapping

By the third month, the goal is a full handover from the project-based pilot team to the business-as-usual managed service structure. Conduct a formal review of all operational procedures and service level agreements with your managed service provider. Ensure your internal team is confident in their roles and responsibilities for day-to-day management, oversight, and escalation.

Collaborate with your provider to develop a strategic six-month roadmap. This plan should outline priority enhancements, such as new feature development or integration with additional enterprise systems. It must be a living document, owned jointly, that aligns the provider’s technical delivery with your evolving business objectives and budget cycles.

The final step is to establish a continuous improvement framework. This involves setting up a structured process for collecting feedback from end-users and feeding it into the development backlog. It also means planning the first major model retraining cycle to combat performance decay. This forward-looking activity ensures the AI asset continues to deliver value long after the initial 90 days.

The handover package must include all operational runbooks and a comprehensive knowledge transfer schedule. Key artefacts are a detailed MLOps pipeline diagram, contact lists for vendor support, and a log of all major incident responses during the pilot. Schedule at least three hands-on workshops where the pilot team walks the sustaining team through model monitoring dashboards, anomaly investigation procedures, and the protocol for retraining triggers. This ensures operational knowledge is embedded, not just documented.

The future roadmap should be prioritised using a value-effort matrix co-developed with stakeholders. For example, 'Integrate with ERP system' might be high value but high effort (Q2 goal), while 'Add email alerting for low-confidence predictions' is low effort and high value (Q1 goal). Each initiative on the roadmap must have a designated business owner from the sustaining team responsible for championing its development and adoption, ensuring the transition from pilot project to core business capability.

The Critical Path: Your 90-Day Transition Timeline

A structured timeline is essential for navigating the transition's complexity. The following roadmap breaks the 90 days into three distinct 30-day phases, each with a clear primary objective. This chronology ensures that foundational stability is achieved before moving to value demonstration and, finally, strategic planning. Adhering to this sequence prevents teams from skipping crucial steps in the rush to scale.

Each phase contains specific, actionable milestones that serve as progress checkpoints. These are not mere suggestions but essential gates that must be passed before proceeding. For instance, the operational procedures must be signed off before attempting any expansion. This disciplined approach de-risks the transition and provides clear markers of success for all stakeholders involved.

This timeline is a guideline that should be tailored to your organisation's specific context and risk appetite. However, the core sequence—stabilise, scale, strategise—remains universally applicable. It provides the necessary cadence to transform a promising pilot into a fully operational and strategically aligned enterprise asset.

Integrate specific deliverables and approval gates into the timeline. At the end of Week 2, a formal 'Foundation Sign-off' meeting with security and compliance is mandatory before any live data processing can begin. The timeline should mandate a 'Value Review' at the end of Month 2, where the business sponsor must formally attest to the achieved KPIs before the team is authorised to proceed with the Month 3 handover activities. These gates institutionalise decision-making and prevent the project from proceeding without validating critical assumptions.

Measuring Success: The Key Performance Indicators

Quantifying progress requires tracking a balanced set of metrics from the very start. These KPIs should cover operational health, business impact, and financial performance. Avoid vanity metrics; every tracked number must inform a specific decision. For example, model accuracy is operational, while reduced manual labour hours is a business impact metric directly tied to value.

Operational KPIs include system uptime, average inference latency, and model accuracy against a held-out test set. Business value KPIs are specific to your pilot's goal, such as a percentage increase in sales conversion or a reduction in average handling time for customer queries. Financial metrics should track the total cost of operation and the return on investment.

Report these metrics weekly to the transition team and monthly to the broader governance committee. Use a simple dashboard that highlights trends and flags any metrics drifting from their agreed baseline. This consistent reporting creates accountability, provides early warning of issues, and builds a compelling data-driven narrative for the programme's success.

Beyond technical metrics, track business process KPIs rigorously. For a predictive maintenance pilot, measure the reduction in mean time to repair (MTTR) and the increase in asset availability percentage. For a targeting system, track the improvement in engagement ratio. Establish a baseline from the 30 days preceding the pilot's launch for accurate comparison. These business-centric KPIs directly translate the AI's performance into operational and financial impact, which is the ultimate language for securing buy-in for full-scale production investment.

Anticipating Challenges: A Decision Framework for Common Hurdles

Despite meticulous planning, challenges will arise. A pre-defined decision framework empowers your team to respond effectively without constant escalation. This matrix categorises potential issues—from technical performance decay to stakeholder resistance—and outlines the agreed response, the owner, and the required timeframe for resolution. It turns reactive firefighting into proactive management.

For instance, a drop in model accuracy might be owned by the technical lead, with a response involving data quality checks and a decision rule: if accuracy drops below a certain threshold for two consecutive days, initiate a predefined retraining process. This clarity speeds up response times and prevents minor issues from becoming major crises.

Regularly review and update this framework with your managed service provider. New challenges will emerge as the system scales, and the playbook must evolve. This living document becomes a core component of your operational resilience, ensuring the service remains robust and reliable as it grows in complexity and importance.

Create a decision framework for common data hurdles. If model accuracy plateaus, the rule might be: first, attempt feature engineering with existing data; if no improvement in two weeks, consult the Data Steward on expanding data sources; if unresolved after one month, escalate to the project steering committee for a decision on investing in new data acquisition. This provides a clear escalation path and action plan, preventing teams from spinning their wheels indefinitely on intractable data problems and ensuring timely decisions.

Building the Right Team: Roles and Responsibilities

A successful transition hinges on clear roles, not just on technology. Your core team should be small, cross-functional, and empowered. The Business Lead represents the sponsoring department, owns the value case, and prioritises the backlog. The Technical Owner, from your IT organisation, ensures compatibility with enterprise architecture and security standards.

The Operations Representative is responsible for integrating the AI's output into daily workflows and supporting end-users. An optional Finance Liaison can help track costs and ROI. This team must meet weekly throughout the 90 days to maintain momentum and alignment. They are the engine of the transition.

Your managed service provider assigns a counterpart to this team: a Delivery Manager. This person is your single point of contact, responsible for the provider's execution, reporting, and issue resolution. The clarity of this joint team structure eliminates ambiguity, ensures accountability, and creates a partnership model that is essential for long-term success.

Clearly define the handover of responsibilities from the pilot team to the sustainment team. The Pilot AI Engineer is responsible for documenting model quirks until the end of Month 3, while the Sustaining ML Ops Engineer shadows them from Month 2 onward and assumes on-call duties starting Week 1 of Month 4. The Business Analyst from the pilot team must deliver a final report of insights to the Product Owner on the sustainment team, ensuring strategic knowledge is transferred alongside operational knowledge.
